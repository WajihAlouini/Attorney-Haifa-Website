**In short: no inheritance equality law has been adopted.** In 2026, estates are still divided under the Personal Status Code (CSP). Here is who inherits what, with worked examples.

## Who inherits what?

In this table, "children" means the deceased's children and the children of the deceased's sons.

| Heir | Share | CSP article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Son | What remains after the fixed shares. With daughters, each son receives twice a daughter's share. | 103, 119 |
| Only daughter, no brother | One half (1/2) | 103 |
| Two or more daughters, no brother | Two thirds (2/3), shared equally | 103 |
| Daughters with one or more brothers | They inherit with their brothers: each daughter receives half a son's share | 103 |
| Wife | 1/4 with no children, 1/8 if there are children | 102 |
| Husband | 1/2 with no children, 1/4 if there are children | 101 |
| Father | 1/6 if there is a son; 1/6 plus the remainder if there are only daughters; all the remainder if there are no children | 99 |
| Mother | 1/6 if there are children or several brothers and sisters; 1/3 otherwise. With the father and a spouse: 1/3 of what remains after the spouse's share | 107 |
| Brothers and sisters | Do not inherit if there is a son, a son's son or the father | 130, 140 |
| Grandchildren of a child who died first | Mandatory bequest: the share their parent would have received, capped at 1/3 | 191, 192 |

## The Tunisian rule that protects daughters

When there is no son, daughters and sons' daughters take the remainder of the estate **even if the deceased leaves brothers, paternal uncles or their descendants** (Article 143 bis). An only daughter therefore does not have to share with her uncles or cousins.

The one exception: if the deceased's father is alive, he receives 1/6 plus the remainder (Article 99).

## Three worked examples

**1. A wife, a son and a daughter.** The wife receives 1/8. The rest is divided between the children, with the son receiving twice the daughter's share:

- Wife: 1/8
- Son: 7/12
- Daughter: 7/24

**2. A husband, a daughter and the deceased's brother; the deceased's father has died.** The husband receives 1/4. The daughter receives 1/2, plus the remainder under Article 143 bis:

- Husband: 1/4
- Daughter: 3/4
- Brother: nothing

**3. A husband and both parents, with no children.** The husband receives 1/2. The mother receives 1/3 of what remains, and the father the balance:

- Husband: 1/2
- Mother: 1/6
- Father: 1/3

These examples assume there are no other heirs. A single additional relative can change the calculation.

## What a will can and cannot do

- **No bequest to an heir** and **no more than one third** of the estate, unless the heirs agree after the death (Article 179).
- **A bequest to someone who is not an heir** takes effect without the heirs' consent if it does not exceed one third (Article 187).
- **A lifetime gift** can transfer an asset to a specific person, for example a daughter. It must follow strict formalities, especially for real estate.

## What about inheritance equality?

- **2018**: the Individual Freedoms and Equality Commission (COLIBE) recommended equal inheritance. The Council of Ministers adopted a bill on 23 November 2018. It made equality the default, with an option to choose the current rules during one's lifetime.
- **2019**: the text was examined in a parliamentary committee, then stalled. It was never put to a vote.
- **Since then**: President Kaïs Saïed has publicly opposed inheritance equality, and the Parliament elected in 2019 was dissolved in 2022.
- **November 2026**: no new law has been adopted. The debate continues mainly within civil society.
